How we work

Five steps to designed comfort

  1. Comfort survey

    We walk every room, log temperatures and ask which spaces you actually avoid in August.

  2. Room-by-room study

    A load calculation per room, plus static pressure on the existing duct work, before any equipment is named.

  3. Design presentation

    One page per option: capacity, sound level, staging behaviour, rebates and the real annual cost.

  4. Installation

    Protected floors, crane and permit scheduling handled, and a named lead technician on site all day.

  5. Commissioning

    Airflow and charge verified, a printed report, and a return visit inside the first cooling season.

Before you commit

Straight answers on designed comfort

Why a room-by-room load study?

Because a whole-house number cannot tell you which rooms are starved. The per-room study is what makes zoning decisions honest.

Is variable-capacity worth it here?

In {{REGION}} heat, usually yes: longer run times at low capacity dehumidify better and cost less than repeated full-speed cycling.

What does the maintenance programme include?

Three visits a year, priority response, refrigerant and electrical trending, and a named technician who knows your system.

Do you handle HOA and permits?

Yes. We submit equipment locations and screening details to your HOA and pull {{CITY}} permits before scheduling the crane.

How far out is installation?

Typically one to two weeks outside peak season, and we keep emergency cooling available for members in the meantime.
